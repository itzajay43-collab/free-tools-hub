What Is EMI?

EMI stands for Equated Monthly Instalment. It is the regular amount a borrower pays toward a loan over an agreed repayment period.

For a typical reducing-balance loan, an EMI payment consists of both a portion of the principal amount and the interest charged by the lender.

What Factors Affect Your EMI?

Three major factors generally determine the EMI for a loan:

  • Loan Principal: The amount borrowed.
  • Interest Rate: The rate charged on the loan.
  • Loan Tenure: The length of the repayment period.

Changing any of these values can change the monthly EMI and the total amount paid over the loan period.

EMI Formula

A common formula for calculating EMI on a reducing-balance loan is:

EMI = P × r × (1 + r)n ÷ ((1 + r)n − 1)

Where:

  • P = Loan principal
  • r = Monthly interest rate in decimal form
  • n = Total number of monthly instalments

If the annual interest rate is given as a percentage, the monthly rate is commonly calculated by dividing the annual rate by 12 and converting the percentage to a decimal.

EMI Calculation Example

Suppose you borrow ₹5,00,000 at an annual interest rate of 10% for 5 years.

Loan amount = ₹5,00,000
Annual interest rate = 10%
Tenure = 5 years
Number of monthly instalments = 60
Monthly interest rate = 10 ÷ 12 ÷ 100

The EMI formula can then be used to calculate the monthly repayment. An online calculator makes this calculation faster and reduces the need for manual mathematical steps.

How Does Loan Tenure Affect EMI?

Loan tenure is the amount of time allowed to repay a loan. A longer tenure generally results in a lower monthly EMI because the repayment is spread across more instalments.

Important: A lower monthly EMI does not necessarily mean a lower overall cost. A longer repayment period can result in more total interest being paid.

How Does Interest Rate Affect EMI?

When the interest rate increases, the EMI generally increases, assuming the loan amount and tenure remain unchanged.

Even a relatively small change in the interest rate can affect the total interest paid over a long repayment period.

Principal vs Interest

The principal is the amount originally borrowed. Interest is the additional amount charged by the lender for providing the loan.

Over the repayment period, each EMI contributes toward both the outstanding principal and the applicable interest. The exact allocation can change throughout the loan tenure.

How to Calculate Total Loan Payment

Once the monthly EMI and number of instalments are known, the total amount paid through EMIs can be calculated as:

Total Payment = EMI × Number of Instalments

The approximate interest paid over the loan period can then be found by subtracting the original principal from the total payment:

Total Interest = Total Payment − Principal

Frequently Asked Questions

What is EMI?

EMI means Equated Monthly Instalment. It is the regular payment made toward a loan, generally including principal and interest.

How is EMI calculated?

A common reducing-balance EMI formula uses the principal, monthly interest rate and total number of monthly instalments.

What factors affect EMI?

Loan amount, interest rate and loan tenure are the main factors affecting EMI.

Does a longer tenure reduce EMI?

Generally, yes. However, a longer tenure can increase the total interest paid over the life of the loan.
