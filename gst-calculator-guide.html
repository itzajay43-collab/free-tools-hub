How to Calculate GST

Learn how GST is calculated, how to add GST to a price and how to calculate the original price from a GST-inclusive amount.

What Is GST?

GST stands for Goods and Services Tax. It is an indirect tax applied to many goods and services.

The applicable GST rate can vary depending on the product or service. Therefore, you should use the rate applicable to your particular transaction when performing a GST calculation.

Important: This guide explains the mathematical calculation of GST. It does not determine which GST rate applies to a particular product or service.

How to Calculate GST

If you know the original taxable price and the applicable GST rate, the GST amount can be calculated using:

GST Amount = Original Price × GST Rate ÷ 100

The GST-inclusive price can then be calculated by adding the GST amount to the original price.

Final Price = Original Price + GST Amount

Example: Adding GST to a Price

Suppose the taxable price of a product is ₹1,000 and the applicable GST rate is 18%.

GST Amount = ₹1,000 × 18 ÷ 100

GST Amount = ₹180

Final Price = ₹1,000 + ₹180
Final Price = ₹1,180

So, at an 18% GST rate, a taxable price of ₹1,000 becomes ₹1,180 after adding GST.

What Is a GST-Inclusive Price?

A GST-inclusive price already contains the GST amount. If you only know the final GST-inclusive amount, you can calculate the original taxable value using the applicable GST rate.

Original Price = Inclusive Price × 100 ÷ (100 + GST Rate)

Example: Removing GST From an Inclusive Price

Suppose the final price is ₹1,180 and it includes GST at 18%.

Original Price = ₹1,180 × 100 ÷ 118

Original Price = ₹1,000

GST Amount = ₹1,180 − ₹1,000
GST Amount = ₹180

Therefore, when ₹1,180 includes 18% GST, the taxable value is ₹1,000 and the GST component is ₹180.

GST Exclusive vs GST Inclusive

  • GST Exclusive: The displayed price does not include GST. GST is added separately.
  • GST Inclusive: The displayed price already includes GST.

Why Is GST Calculation Useful?

GST calculations can be useful when checking invoices, estimating final prices, comparing taxable and tax-inclusive amounts, or calculating the tax component of a transaction.
